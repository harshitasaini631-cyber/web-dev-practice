<!-- CSS units are divided into two main categories based on how they behave :
● Absolute Units.
● Relative Units.

Absolute units are fixed sizes.
They do not change no matter what screen size or device you’re using.
Example : Rulers in real life — 1 cm is always 1 cm, no matter where you are.

1. px (Pixels): A pixel is the tiniest dot your screen can show.

It's the most commonly used absolute unit in web design.
Example: font-size: 16px; → sets the text size to 16 screen dots.

2. in (inches): 1 inch is the same as on a physical ruler.
Mostly used for print styles, not for screens.
Example: width: 2in; → makes an element 2 inches wide when printed.

3. cm (Centimeter): 1 cm = 10 millimeters.

Also used mainly for printing, not screen design.
Example: margin: 1cm; → gives 1 cm space around the
element.

4. mm (Millimeters): Even smaller than cm — 10 mm = 1 cm.

Rarely used, but good for very precise print layouts.
Example: padding: 5mm; → adds 5 mm space inside an element.

5. pt (Points): 1 point = 1/72 of an inch.

Common in Word documents and print design.
Example: font-size: 12pt; → standard paragraph text size in
documents.

6. pc (Picas): 1 pica = 12 points.

Used in typesetting (like newspapers or books).
Very rare in web design.-->
